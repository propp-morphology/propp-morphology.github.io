The morphology of the wonder tale: 31 functions

In Morphology of the Folktale (1928), Vladimir Propp analysed one hundred Russian wonder tales from Afanasyev's collection and identified 31 functions. “Function is understood as an act of a character, defined from the point of view of its significance for the course of the action.” (1968, p. 21) Each function has a symbol, so the structure of a tale can be written as a formula.

The basic scheme

Thirty-one functions in a constant sequence

Not every function occurs in every tale, but Propp holds their sequence to be constant (thesis III). He accounts for the departures himself, as an “inverted sequence” and as functions that change places (1968, p. 107). The coloured stripe marks the sphere of action to which Propp assigns a function (1968, pp. 79–80). The six groups are this page's division of the scheme, not Propp's categories.

The distribution of functions among dramatis personae

Seven dramatis personae

Propp defines the dramatis personae through spheres of action, the groups of functions that one character carries out (1968, pp. 79–80). The spheres overlap: the false hero shares C, ↑ and E with the hero. One character can cover several spheres, and one sphere can be divided among several characters.

Moves

Each new act of villainy or lack creates a new move

A move runs from a villainy (A) or a lack (a) to its liquidation or another ending. Propp names six ways in which moves combine (1968, p. 93).

Propp's tales

Propp's 13 worked analyses

Each move is mapped onto the basic scheme; below it is the tale's formula in Propp's notation. Functions describe the structural basis of a tale, not all its elements: motivations, connectives and the attributes of characters lie outside the scheme. Titles are given in English with the Russian original: three as in the 1968 edition, the others our glosses; the tale texts and Propp's analyses exist in Russian, and the symbols are converted to the 1968 notation.
